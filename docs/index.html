Beyond Clicks: Inferring Decision Uncertainty from Mouse Behaviour for Proactive AI Assistance

Can a website notice from your mouse movement that you are stuck choosing, and offer the right help at the right moment, so that you decide faster without deciding worse?

Code on GitHub Presentation (PDF) Video summary

Abstract

Recommender systems use what people bought or liked before, but nothing helps them while they are stuck choosing between options. Mouse-tracking research shows that conflict between options is visible in cursor movement. We built a web experiment in which participants choose the best phone for a given buyer. A real-time detector combines five cursor signs (back-and-forth between phones, freezing on a spec, pausing, wandering and scroll flip-flops), each compared with the person's own baseline. When the detector sees hesitation, an LLM assistant offers a comparison or a spec explanation grounded in the phone data.

The detector's threshold is chosen on pilot data, against participants' own replay labels, with a pre-registered rule and leave-one-person-out validation. It is then frozen. In the main within-subjects study (balanced Latin square), we compare four conditions: no AI, on-demand AI, detector-timed pop-ups and randomly timed pop-ups. The measures are decision time, decision quality (rank of the chosen phone, correctness, switching), whether people were really unsure when the pop-up appeared, workload and trust.

Method in brief

PhaseWhat happens
Pilot (8–12 people)Warm-ups for a personal baseline, 4 rounds without AI, then a replay of one's own cursor while holding SPACE when one remembers feeling unsure.
FreezeSimulate the live detector on the pilot data; pick the threshold by a pre-registered rule; validate with leave-one-person-out; lock all settings.
Main (24 people)4 rounds: No AI, Ask AI, Smart pop-up, Random pop-up, in a counterbalanced order; questionnaires after each round (NASA-TLX, SAM, trust, SUS).
